PnP-FISTA

发表于2026-08-19|更新于2026-08-19
|浏览量:

$$\min_{x\in \mathbb R^n} (f(x))+(g(x))$$ $(f(x))$ smooth $(g(x))$ non‑smooth

FISTA为 $$x_k=\text{prox}_{\gamma g}\big(y_k-\gamma\nabla f(y_k)\big)$$ $$t_{k+1}=\frac12\big(1+\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}\big)$$ $$y_{k+1}=x_k+\frac{t_k-1}{t_{k+1}}(x_k-x_{k-1})$$

PnP‑FISTA 将 prox operator 变为由数据驱动的去噪器 $W$ $$x_k=W\big(y_k-\gamma\nabla f(y_k)\big)$$

$$t_{k+1}=\frac12\big(1+\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}\big)$$ $$y_{k+1}=x_k+\frac{t_k-1}{t_{k+1}}(x_k-x_{k-1})$$

$x\to W(x)x$ 其中 $W$ 由训练集 $X_g$ 生成，在此假设为线性算子 $$x\to Wx$$

为证明 PnP‑FISTA 的收敛不一定收敛，面设更一般化的 $\alpha$-PnP‑FISTA 形式 $$x_k=W\big(y_k-\gamma\nabla f(y_k)\big)$$ $$y_{k+1}=x_k+\alpha_k(x_k-x_{k-1})$$ $$\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_k=1$$

则 PnP‑FISTA 为 $\alpha$-PnP‑FISTA 一特例 $\alpha_k=\frac{t_k-1}{t_{k+1}}$

proof $$\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}\ge 2t_k$$ $$t_{k+1}\ge t_k+\frac12$$ So $t_k\ge\frac k2$ $\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}t_k=+\infty$

$$\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_k=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{t_k-1}{t_{k+1}} =\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{t_k-1}{\frac12\big(1+\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}\big)} =\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{2\big(1-\frac{1}{t_k}\big)}{\frac{1}{t_k}+\sqrt{4+\frac{1}{t_k^2}}}=\frac{2}{2}=1$$

设 $(f(x))=\frac12\|Ax-b\|^2$

Assumption: $\sigma(W)\subset[0,1]$ 且 $\ker(A)\cap\mathrm{fix}(w)=\{0\}$ 即去噪器 $W$ 不会 某一方向的向量 同时也不存在非零向量 $x$ 使 $A(x=0),\;Wx=x$ 即对代价无单影响却被 $W$ 保留

lemma1.(Schur) For $A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$ with eigenvalues $\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_n$ $\exists$ a unitary matrix $U\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$ s.t. $U^TAU=T=[t_{ij}]$ $T$ is upper triangular and $t_{ii}=\lambda_i\quad i=1\cdots n$

proof: 对 $A$ 的特征值 $\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_n$ 找到 $\varepsilon_1$ 为 $\lambda_1$ 对应单位特征向量 将将 $\varepsilon_1$ 扩为一组标准正交基 $\{\varepsilon_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3\cdots\alpha_n\}$

设 $U_1=(\varepsilon_1\;\alpha_2\;\cdots\alpha_n)$

$$ U_1^TAU_1= \begin{pmatrix} \varepsilon_1^T\\ \alpha_2^T\\ \vdots\\ \alpha_n^T \end{pmatrix} \cdot (A\varepsilon_1,\;A\alpha_2\;\cdots A\alpha_n) = \begin{pmatrix} \varepsilon_1^T\\ \alpha_2^T\\ \vdots\\ \alpha_n^T \end{pmatrix} \cdot (\lambda_1\varepsilon_1,\;A\alpha_2\;\cdots A\alpha_n) = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & *\\ 0 & A_1 \end{bmatrix} $$

$$ |A-\lambda I|= \begin{vmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & *\\ 0 & A_1 \end{bmatrix}-\lambda I \end{vmatrix} = \begin{vmatrix} \lambda_1-\lambda & *\\ 0 & A_1-\lambda I \end{vmatrix} =(\lambda_1-\lambda)|A_1-\lambda I| =(\lambda_1-\lambda)(\lambda_2-\lambda)\cdots(\lambda_n-\lambda) $$

So $|A_1-\lambda I|=(\lambda_2-\lambda)(\lambda_3-\lambda)\cdots(\lambda_n-\lambda)$ $A_1$ 的特征值为 $\lambda_2\cdots\lambda_n$

重复上述步骤得到 $$U_2^TU_1^TAU_1U_2= \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & * & *\\ 0 & \lambda_2 & *\\ 0 & 0 & A_2 \end{bmatrix} $$

令 $U=U_1U_2\cdots U_{n-1}$ $U$ 为 unitary matrix，则 $U^TAU=T$，$T$ is upper triangular and $t_{ii}=\lambda_i$

lemma2. Let $A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$，$\forall \varepsilon>0$ $\exists$ a matrix norm s.t. $\rho(A)\le\|A\|\le\rho(A)+\varepsilon$ $\rho(A)$ is the largest eigenvalue of $A$

proof By lemma1. $\exists$ unitary matrix $U$ and upper triangular matrix $\Delta$ s.t. $A=U^T\Delta U$

Let $D_t=\mathrm{diag}(t,t^2,\cdots t^n)$

$$D_t\Delta D_t^{-1}= \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & t^{-1}d_{12} & t^{-2}d_{13} & \cdots & t^{-n+1}d_{1n}\\ & \lambda_2 & t^{-1}d_{23} & \cdots & t^{-n+2}d_{2n}\\ & & \ddots & \vdots\\ 0 & & & & \lambda_n \end{bmatrix} $$

$\|\cdot\|_1$ is the maximum column sum

So $\|D_t\Delta D_t^{-1}\|_1\le\rho(A)+\varepsilon$ for large enough $t$

define the matrix norm $\|\cdot\|$ by $$\|B\|=\|D_t(U^TBU)D_t^{-1}\|_1=\|(UD_t^{-1})^{-1}B(UD_t^{-1})\|_1$$

So $$\|A\|=\|D_tU^TAUD_t^{-1}\|_1 =\|D_t\Delta D_t^{-1}\|_1\le\rho(A)+\varepsilon$$

$\lambda$ is the eigenvalue of $A$，$\exists$ eigenvector $x\neq0$ s.t. $Ax=\lambda x$

Set nonzero vector $y$，define $X=xy^T$

$$AX=Axy^T=\lambda xy^T=\lambda X$$

So $\|AX\|=\|\lambda X\|=|\lambda|\|X\|$

$\|AX\|\le\|A\|\cdot\|X\|$

$|\lambda|\|X\|\le\|A\|\cdot\|X\|$

$|\lambda|\le\|A\|$

So $\rho(A)\le\|A\|$

So $\rho(A)\le\|A\|\le\rho(A)+\varepsilon$

lemma3. Suppose we are given $s\in\mathbb R^d$ and a sequence of linear operators $\{R_k\}_{k\ge1}\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ $\forall\ k\ge1$ define the affine map $T_k:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^d,\;T_k(z)=R_kz+s$. Suppose $\exists R_\infty\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ s.t. $1^\circ\ \rho(R_\infty)<1$ $2^\circ\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}R_k=R_\infty$ $3^\circ\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^\infty\mathrm{fix}(T_k)\neq\emptyset$ Then , there exist an unique $z^*\in\mathbb R^d$ s.t. $\forall z_0\in\mathbb R^d$ the sequence $\{z_k\}_{k\ge0}$ generated via $z_{k+1}=T_k(z_k)$ converges linearly to $z^*$

proof: Fix $\beta<1$ s.t. $\rho(R_\infty)<\beta$

Via lemma2. $\exists$ a matrix norm $\|\cdot\|_\beta\;\forall\varepsilon>0$ s.t. $\rho(A)\le\|A\|_\beta\le\rho(A)+\varepsilon$

let $\varepsilon=\beta-\rho(R_\infty)$ then $$\rho(R_\infty)\le\|R_\infty\|_\beta\le\beta$$

$$\|B\|_\beta:=\|(UD_t^{-1})^{-1}B(UD_t^{-1})\|_1 =\|Q^{-1}BQ\|_1,\quad Q \text{ is nonsingular}$$

Set vector norm $\|b\|_\beta=\|Q^{-1}b\|_1$ , then the induced matrix norm is $$\|B\|_\beta=\sup_{x\neq0}\frac{\|Bx\|_\beta}{\|x\|_\beta} =\sup_{x\neq0}\frac{\|Q^{-1}Bx\|_1}{\|Q^{-1}x\|_1}$$

Set $y=Q^{-1}x,\ x=Qy$ $$\|B\|_\beta=\sup_{y\neq0}\frac{\|Q^{-1}BQy\|_1}{\|y\|_1}=\|Q^{-1}BQ\|_1$$

So $\|B\|_\beta$ is the induced matrix norm by $\|b\|_\beta$

Now, since $\|R_k-R_\infty\|_\beta\to0,\ \exists K,\ \forall k\ge K\ \|R_k\|_\beta\le\beta$

Let $z^*\in\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^\infty\mathrm{fix}(T_k)$

$$z_{k+1}-z^*=T_k(z_k)-T_k(z^*)=R_k(z_k-z^*)$$

$$\|R_k(z_k-z^*)\|_\beta\le\|R_k\|_\beta\cdot\|z_k-z^*\|_\beta$$

for $\forall k\ge K$ $$\|R_k(z_k-z^*)\|_\beta\le\beta\cdot\|z_k-z^*\|_\beta$$

$$\|z_{k+1}-z^*\|_\beta\le\beta\cdot\|z_k-z^*\|_\beta \le\beta^{k+1-K}\cdot\|z_K-z^*\|_\beta=C\cdot\beta^{k+1}$$

$C$ is not depend on $k$ so $\{z_k\}$ converges linearly to $z^*$ for any initialization $z_0$

And since $z^*=T_kz^*$ $$z^*=R_kz^*+s$$

let $k\to\infty$ $z^*=R_\infty z^*+s$

$$(I_d-R_\infty)z^*=s$$

$\rho(R_\infty)<1$ so $1$ is not the eigenvalue of $R_\infty$ so $I-R_\infty$ is nonsingular. and $z^*$ is unique

So $\bigcap\limits_{k=1}^\infty\mathrm{fix}(T_k)$ is singleton

Prop 1. Let $P\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ $\sigma(P)\subset[0,1)$, If $R\in\mathbb R^{2n\times2n}$ is defined to be $$R=\begin{pmatrix}2P & -P\\ I_n & O_n\end{pmatrix}$$ then $\rho(R)<1$

proof: Let $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ be a nonzero eigenvalue of $R$ $\exists\ x=\begin{pmatrix}z_1\\z_2\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{2n},\ z_1,z_2\in\mathbb R^n$ $$Rx=\lambda x$$

$$ \begin{pmatrix} 2P & -P\\ I & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} z_1\\ z_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda z_1\\ \lambda z_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2Pz_1-Pz_2\\ z_1 \end{pmatrix} $$

So $2Pz_1-Pz_2=\lambda z_1$ $z_1=\lambda z_2$

If $\lambda=\frac12$ or $z_1=0$ then $(x=0)$ , So assume $\lambda\neq\frac12,\ z_1\neq0$

$$2Pz_1-P\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda}z_1=\lambda z_1$$ $$2\lambda Pz_1-Pz_1=\lambda^2 z_1$$ $$(2\lambda-1)Pz_1=\lambda^2 z_1$$ $$Pz_1=\frac{\lambda^2}{2\lambda-1}z_1$$

Set $\mu=\frac{\lambda^2}{2\lambda-1}$ then $Pz_1=\mu z_1$

Since $z_1\neq0$ so $\mu$ is the eigenvalue of $P$ and $$\lambda^2=2\mu\lambda-\mu$$ $$\lambda^2-2\mu\lambda+\mu=0$$

$$\lambda=\frac{2\mu\pm\sqrt{4\mu^2-4\mu}}{2}=\mu\pm\sqrt{\mu^2-\mu}$$

$\mu\in\sigma(P)\subset[0,1)$ so $\lambda=\mu\pm i\sqrt{\mu-\mu^2}$

$$|\lambda|=\sqrt{\mu^2+\mu-\mu^2}=\sqrt{\mu}<1$$

So $\rho(R)<1$

Prop 2. Let $M\in S^n$ and $\sigma(M)\subset[0,1]$ Then $\|Mx\|_2=\|x\|_2$ iff $(Mx=x)$

proof $\Rightarrow$ $M$ is symmetric matrix so $\exists$ orthogonal matrix s.t. $M=Q\Lambda Q^T$ $\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)\ i=1\cdots n\ \lambda_i\in[0,1]$

Set $y=Q^Tx$

$$ \begin{aligned} \|Mx\|_2^2&=\|M^2x\|_2=x^TQ\Lambda^2Q^Tx\\ &=y^T\Lambda^2y\\ &=\sum_i\lambda_i^2y_i^2 \le\sum_i y_i^2=\|y\|_2^2=\|Q^Tx\|_2^2=\|x\|_2^2 \end{aligned} $$

equality holds iff $\lambda_i=1$ or $(y=0)$

$1^\circ\ (y=0)$ means $(x=0)$ and $M(x=0)$ So $(Mx=x)$

$2^\circ\ \lambda_i=1$ $$Mx=Q\Lambda Q^TQy=Q\Lambda y=Qy=x$$

$\Leftarrow$ is obviously

lemma4 For $W\in S^n$ and $A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ set satisfying Assumption, define $$P_\gamma:=W(I-\gamma A^TA)$$ Then $\sigma(P_\gamma)\subset[0,1)$ for all $\gamma\in(0,1/\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A^TA))$

proof Define $G_\gamma=I_n-\gamma A^TA\in S^n$ $\sigma(G_\gamma)\subset[0,1]$ for $0<\gamma<1/\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A^TA)$

$\sigma(G_\gamma)=\{1-\gamma\lambda_i\}\quad \lambda_i=\sigma(A^TA)$

$$0<\gamma<\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A^TA)}$$

$$0\ge-\gamma\lambda_i>-\frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}}$$

$$1-\frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}}<1-\gamma\lambda_i\le1$$

So $\sigma(G_\gamma)\subset[0,1]$ and $G_\gamma$ is symmetric positive‑semidefinite

So $\exists\ G_\gamma=Q\Lambda Q^T$

And let $G_\gamma^{\frac12}=Q\Lambda^{\frac12}Q^T\in S^n$

$$P_\gamma=W G_\gamma$$

$$=G_\gamma^{-\frac12}\big(G_\gamma^{\frac12}W G_\gamma^{\frac12}\big)G_\gamma^{\frac12}$$

So $P_\gamma \sim G_\gamma^{\frac12}W G_\gamma^{\frac12}$

Since $\sigma(W)\subset[0,1]$ so $P_\gamma$ is positive‑semidefinite

$\sigma(P_\gamma)\subset[0,+\infty)$

$$\rho(P_\gamma)=\rho(P_\gamma)\frac{\|x\|_2}{\|x\|_2}=\frac{\|P_\gamma x\|_2}{\|x\|_2}\le\max_{\|x\|_2=1}\|P_\gamma x\|_2$$

We need prove $\forall\|x\|_2=1\quad\|P_\gamma x\|_2<1$

Since $W,\ G_\gamma\in S$ so $\|W\|_2=\rho(W)\le1$ $\|G_\gamma\|_2=\rho(G_\gamma)\le1$

let $x\in\mathbb R^n,\|x\|_2=1$

$1^\circ x\in\ker(A)\quad 2^\circ x\notin\ker(A)$

Case $1^\circ$

Since $\ker(A)\cap\mathrm{fix}(W)=\{0\}$

So $x\notin\mathrm{fix}(W)\iff Wx\neq x$

by Prop2. $\|Wx\|_2\neq\|x\|_2$

Since $\|Wx\|_2\le\|W\|_2\|x\|_2\Rightarrow\|Wx\|_2\le\|x\|_2$

So $\|Wx\|_2<\|x\|_2$

$G_\gamma=I_n-\gamma A^TA$ $G_\gamma x=x$

So $\|P_\gamma x\|_2=\|WG_\gamma x\|_2\le\|Wx\|_2<\|x\|_2=1$

Case $2^\circ$

$Ax\neq0$ so $G_\gamma x\neq x$ , by Prop2. $\|G_\gamma x\|_2\neq\|x\|_2$

Similar $\|G_\gamma x\|_2<\|x\|_2$

So $\|P_\gamma x\|_2=\|WG_\gamma x\|_2\le\|G_\gamma x\|_2<\|x\|_2=1$

So $\sigma(P_\gamma)\subset[0,1)$

Theorem For the $\alpha$-PnP‑FISTA with $(f(x))=\frac12\|Ax-b\|_2^2$ $W$ and $A$ set satisfy assumption. Then the iterates exhibit global linear convergence for any $\gamma\in(0,1/\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A^TA))$

proof $$x_k=W\big(y_k-\gamma\nabla f(y_k)\big)$$ $$y_{k+1}=x_k+\alpha_k(x_k-x_{k-1})$$ $$\lim_{k\to\infty}\alpha_k=1$$

$$P_\gamma=W(I-\gamma A^TA)$$

$$ \begin{aligned} x_k&=Wy_k-W\gamma A^T(Ay_k-b)\\ &=W(I-\gamma A^TA)y_k+W\gamma A^Tb\\ &=P_\gamma y_k+q \end{aligned} $$

$$x_k=P_\gamma\big(x_{k-1}+\alpha_{k-1}(x_{k-1}-x_{k-2})\big)+q$$ $$=(1+\alpha_{k-1})P_\gamma x_{k-1}-\alpha_{k-1}P_\gamma x_{k-2}+q$$

For $k\ge1$ define $$z_k=\begin{pmatrix}x_k\\x_{k-1}\end{pmatrix}$$

Since $\ker(A)\cap\mathrm{fix}(W)=\{0\}\ \gamma\in(0,1/\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A^TA))$

by lemma4. $\rho(P_\gamma)<1$ so $I-P_\gamma$ is nonsingular

$$x^*=(I_n-P_\gamma)^{-1}q\quad z^*=\begin{pmatrix}x^*\\x^*\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{2n}$$

$z_k=T_k(z_{k-1})$ $T_kz=R_kz+s\quad s=\binom{q}{0}\ R_k=\begin{pmatrix}(1+\alpha_k)P_\gamma & -\alpha_kP_\gamma\\ I & 0\end{pmatrix}$

$$R_\infty=\begin{pmatrix}2P_\gamma & -P_\gamma\\ I & 0\end{pmatrix}$$

by prop1. $\rho(R_\infty)<1$

$$\lim_{k\to\infty}R_k=R_\infty$$

$$T_kz^*=\binom{P_\gamma x^*+x^*-P_\gamma x^*}{x^*}=z^*\quad \text{so}\ \mathrm{fix}(T_k)\neq\emptyset$$

by lemma3. $\exists\beta\in[0,1)\ C>0$ norm $\|\cdot\|$ on $\mathbb R^{2n},\ K\ge1$ s.t. $\forall k\ge K\ \|z_k-z^*\|\le C\beta^k$

$$\|z_k-z^*\| =\left\|\binom{x_k-x^*}{x_{k-1}-x^*}\right\|$$

So $\|x_k-x^*\|\le C\|z_k-z^*\|$

So $\{x_k\}_{k\ge1}$ converges linearly to $x^*$

If $W$ is nonsymmetric like $W_{\mathrm{NLM}}=D^{-1}K$ $D=\mathrm{diag}(K1)\in S$

we need define $\langle x,y\rangle_D=x^TDy$

Then $$ \begin{aligned} x_k&=W_{\mathrm{NLM}}\big(y_k-\gamma\nabla_D f(y_k)\big)\\ &=W_{\mathrm{NLM}}\big(y_k-\gamma D^{-1}A^T(Ay_k-b)\big)=P_\gamma y_k+q \end{aligned} $$

$$P_\gamma=W_{\mathrm{NLM}}\big(I_n-\gamma D^{-1}A^TA\big)\quad q=\gamma W_{\mathrm{NLM}}D^{-1}A^Tb$$

Then let $\gamma\in\big(0,1/\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(D^{-1/2}A^TAD^{-1/2})\big)$

Since $D_{ii}\ge1$ so $\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(D^{-1/2}A^TAD^{-1/2})\le\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A^TA)$

So $\big(0,\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(A^TA)}\big)\subseteq\big(0,\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(D^{-1/2}A^TAD^{-1/2})}\big)$

$$ \begin{aligned} P_\gamma &\sim D^{\frac12}P_\gamma D^{-\frac12}=D^{\frac12}D^{-1}K\big(I_n-\gamma D^{-1}A^TA\big)D^{-\frac12}\\ &=D^{-\frac12}K\big(D^{-\frac12}-\gamma D^{-1}A^TAD^{-\frac12}\big)\\ &=D^{-\frac12}KD^{-\frac12}\big(I_n-\gamma D^{-\frac12}A^TAD^{-\frac12}\big) \end{aligned} $$

let $W_S=D^{-\frac12}KD^{-\frac12}$ $G_\gamma=I_n-\gamma D^{-\frac12}A^TAD^{-\frac12}$ $P_\gamma\sim W_SG_\gamma$ $\sigma(P_\gamma)=\sigma(W_SG_\gamma)$ $W_S,G_\gamma\in S$

$W_S\sim W_{\mathrm{NLM}}$ so $\sigma(W_S)\subset[0,1]$

$$ \begin{aligned} \mathrm{fix}(W_S)\cap\mathrm{fix}(G_\gamma)&=\big(D^{\frac12}\mathrm{fix}(W_{\mathrm{NLM}})\big)\cap\big(D^{\frac12}\ker(A)\big)\\ &=C\cap\ker(A)=\{0\} \end{aligned} $$

So by lemma4. $\sigma(P_\gamma)\subset[0,1)$
